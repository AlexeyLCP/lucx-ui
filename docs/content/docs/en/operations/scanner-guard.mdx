The panel can ban the subnets that probe VPN panels and inbounds — the RKN and
government/scanner ranges the community lists track (the same model as
`rkn-guard` or remnanode's RKN mode, but list-based only: your clients are
never at risk of a false ban).

## Requirements

- Linux host with `iptables` and `ipset` (`apt install ipset` / the
  distribution equivalent — the page shows a hint when `ipset` is missing).
- The panel process needs rights to manage netfilter (root or the shipped
  systemd unit's capabilities).

## Use it

1. Open **Scanner Guard** (`/scanguard`), review the default list source
   (shadow-netlab `government_networks.list`) and press **Save & apply** with
   the toggle on.
2. The panel fetches every source, parses `one CIDR or IP per line`, drops
   subnets that are too broad or private, subtracts your whitelist and
   **atomically swaps** the `lucx-sg4` / `lucx-sg6` ipset sets.
3. One owner-marked INPUT rule per family (`--comment lucx-scanguard`) drops
   **new** connections from those subnets. Established sessions are not
   touched, so a live SSH from a listed subnet survives a list refresh.
4. The reconcile job re-applies rules lost to reboot or an iptables flush
   (fail2ban/docker) and re-fetches the lists at most once per 6 hours.

## Safety properties

- A broken or unreachable source aborts the refresh and keeps the previous
  blocklist protection is never silently dropped.
- Subnets wider than /8 (v4) or /32 (v6), plus private/loopback/link-local
  ranges, are filtered out before install.
- Your own subnet: put it into **Whitelist** — exact or contained CIDRs are
  excluded. SSH is never port-blocked by design.
- Docker bridge (FORWARD) traffic is not covered — published container ports
  need a container-side firewall.
- Disabling sweeps only the `lucx-scanguard`-marked rules and destroys the
  panel's own sets; no other rule is ever touched.
